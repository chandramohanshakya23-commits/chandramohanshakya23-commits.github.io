Hydrology · Energy · Quantum computation

Water, energy and computation in one open-system view.

ShakyaQX is a research theme for Earth-system science. Its foundation is the SQX manifold, a peer-reviewed, three-dimensional generalisation of the Budyko hypothesis for open hydrological systems. The theme builds from there towards coupled water–energy behaviour, land-surface information and quantum-enhanced computation.

The ShakyaQX theme

More than one paper: a research programme

The published SQX manifold is the first layer. ShakyaQX is meant to grow from it into a connected framework for water, energy and computation in open Earth systems. Each layer below is labelled honestly by its current status.

Published foundation

Open-system hydrology

The SQX manifold makes basin openness an explicit coordinate of the Budyko framework. This covers glacier melt, groundwater depletion, reservoir regulation and inter-basin transfers, which classical theory assumes away.

A dual Budyko view that links how water is partitioned with how surface energy is partitioned, so a basin's hydrological state and thermal state can be read together.

Forward estimation of openness from land-use and land-cover information, together with spatial (GIS) tools that make the framework usable for water planning.

The framework

From a curve to a manifold

Classical Budyko-type equations describe how precipitation splits into evaporation and runoff in a closed catchment at steady state. Real basins are often open: groundwater is pumped or recharged, storage drains after the monsoon, and water moves across boundaries. SQX makes that openness an explicit state variable instead of treating it as error.

The SQX partition equation
E/P = x·[ 1 + (x/φ)γ ]−1/γ
with the generalised water balance  P + ΔS = E + Q
xAridity index, PET / P (the climatic coordinate) φOpenness coordinate, φ = 1 + ΔS/P  (φ > 0). φ = 1 for a closed basin γLandscape response parameter that indexes the family of surfaces E/PEvaporative index; runoff follows as Q/P = φ − E/P
1

Reduces to the classical form

At φ = 1 the surface becomes the Mezentsev–Choudhury–Yang (MCY) Budyko curve, so all classical results are kept as a special case.

2

Physically bounded

Evaporation is limited by energy (E/P → x) in humid climates and by available water, now including storage supply (E/P → φ), in arid ones.

3

Structurally stable

Strictly monotonic and globally concave in x. The input-rescaling singularity is moved to the boundary of the admissible domain (φ → 0) rather than appearing inside it. One continuous equation covers storage-subsidized and storage-depleted states, with no piecewise forms.

4

A stable climatic coordinate

Openness enters as a separate axis, so the aridity index keeps its climatic meaning instead of being rescaled by storage terms.

Sign convention. SQX treats ΔS as a net subsidy to the active water balance. ΔS > 0 is a net water gain (for example glacier melt or groundwater inflow), so φ > 1 is storage-subsidized. ΔS < 0 is depletion (for example groundwater extraction or drought), so φ < 1 is storage-depleted. This differs from the usual storage-anomaly convention, so φ should not be read as a direct restatement of GRACE storage change.

Empirical validation

Tested on the Brahmaputra at Goalpara

The framework was checked against observed discharge at the Goalpara gauging station, on the Brahmaputra for 2002–2021, using GRACE/GRACE-FO storage and TerraClimate climate data. It was then tested independently on seven basins in Myanmar. The results show both where SQX adds value and where it does not.

Where SQX adds value

  • Monthly scale, Goalpara: over the 63 admissible months, SQX reaches NSE 0.650, while the classical φ = 1 form reaches −0.005. The effective monthly γ is about 0.68.
  • Robustness: the result holds under leave-one-out cross-validation (NSE 0.476) and under GRACE Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation (2,000 realisations; mean NSE 0.603, 90% interval 0.16–0.90).
  • Diagnostics: the admissibility violations in 2010, 2013 and 2017 coincide with anomalously large positive ΔS. This is consistent with GRACE leakage from nearby glacierised Himalayan terrain, so SQX also helps flag data problems.

Where it does not, yet

  • Annual scale: at Goalpara, φ only ranges from 0.92 to 1.09, and SQX shows no predictive advantage (NSE −1.407 against −0.601 for the classical form).
  • Irrawaddy–Chindwin, Myanmar: across seven GRDC-gauged basins, SQX does not outperform the classical form in six. Diagnostic tests trace this to numerical conditioning of φ = 1 + ΔS/P under low dry-season precipitation, not to basin size, noise or a failure of the openness concept.
  • Domain: September–December at Goalpara and severely storage-depleted states (φ ≤ 0) lie outside the present equilibrium formulation.

Transferability across basins

An independent test on seven Irrawaddy–Chindwin basins in Myanmar defines an explicit boundary condition: the present φ = 1 + ΔS/P form is poorly conditioned when dry-season precipitation is low.

Reported in Journal of Hydrology 679 (2026) 136406
Future direction

Beyond ΔS/P: the ω–θ openness

Estimating openness as φ = 1 + ω − θ from independent land-surface information, so that it does not inherit the 1/P sensitivity. The plan also includes dynamic γ, routing-corrected φ and tests on more glacier-fed basins.
